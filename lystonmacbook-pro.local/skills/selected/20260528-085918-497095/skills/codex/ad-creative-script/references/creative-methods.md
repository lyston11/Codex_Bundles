# Creative Methods

## Brief Diagnosis

Use this grid before ideation:

| Field | Ask / infer |
| --- | --- |
| Product truth | What can the product credibly do or symbolize? |
| Audience pressure | What does the audience feel but rarely say? |
| Category cliche | What does every competitor keep showing? |
| Cultural friction | What social change, habit, fear, or aspiration surrounds this category? |
| Brand role | Is the brand a helper, challenger, witness, maker, host, or myth-builder? |
| Proof | What concrete detail makes the promise believable? |
| Mandatories | Logo, pack, claim, legal, CTA, retail, duration, channel. |

## Insight Sharpening

Weak insight: "Parents want the best for children."  
Sharper insight: "Parents do not just buy nutrition; they buy a quieter minute inside their own doubt."

Weak insight: "Young people want convenience."  
Sharper insight: "They do not want faster chores; they want chores to stop interrupting the version of themselves they are trying to become."

Use this formula:

```text
People say they want X,
but in the moment they actually feel Y,
because Z keeps happening.
The brand can reframe that moment as A.
```

## Creative Leap Patterns

### 1. Literalize The Invisible

Make an invisible benefit visible as a physical phenomenon.

Examples:
- Trust becomes a soft architecture holding a family together.
- Freshness becomes a tiny weather system inside the package.
- Speed becomes all clocks in the room taking a break.

### 2. Reverse The Category

Do the opposite of the category default.

Examples:
- Instead of loud performance claims, show a product so reliable the world becomes quiet.
- Instead of perfect families, show imperfect moments made manageable.
- Instead of a heroic product shot, let the product be the small object that changes a giant scene.

### 3. Create A Ritual

Invent a repeatable behavior people could remember or copy.

Examples:
- A family "confidence check" before bedtime.
- A commuter's one-second pocket tap before entering the city.
- A table ritual where everyone passes the product like a tiny baton.

### 4. Build A Mini Myth

Give the product world rules and a mythic image, while keeping the benefit clear.

Examples:
- Every time the product is used, a tiny hidden crew repairs the day.
- A child carries a glowing "future map" powered by everyday care.
- A city has secret doors that open only when people are prepared.

### 5. Make The Audience The Hero

Let the product enable, witness, or amplify the audience's agency.

Avoid: "The product saves you."  
Prefer: "You were already doing the hard part; the product gives that effort form."

### 6. Find The End-Frame Twist

Design the final image first. The strongest ads often leave one image that explains everything.

Good end frames:
- Product and benefit in one visual sentence.
- A changed world that only makes sense because of the product.
- A line that reinterprets the whole film.

### 7. Genre Transplant

Borrow the structure of a different genre without turning the ad into parody.

Examples:
- Milk powder as a courtroom cross-examination of parental doubt.
- Perfume as a heist where the stolen object is attention.
- Cold medicine as a malfunctioning broadcast control room.
- B2B AI as a backstage rehearsal before a public decision.

### 8. Proof Method Swap

Change how the product truth is proven.

Instead of:
- Expert explains.
- Parent reassures.
- Product sits in a beautiful scene.

Use:
- Inspection, trial, receipt, map, timestamp, contradiction, public behavior, failed substitute, object witness, or repeated ritual.

### 9. POV Displacement

Move the point of view away from the expected user.

Examples:
- The product seal watches the morning routine.
- The store shelf hears every family argument.
- A spoon becomes the only object that sees parental doubt.
- A delivery note tracks care across a city.

### 10. Product As Standard

The product is not a helper; it becomes the standard other things are measured against.

Examples:
- A family tests advice against the product routine.
- A classroom of objects has to pass the brand's standard.
- A city noise is lowered to the level where the child can sleep.

### 11. Counter-Category Opening

Open with the opposite of the category's expected image.

Examples:
- Milk powder starts in a messy debate, not a soft kitchen.
- Organic starts with mud and documentation, not a perfect sunbeam.
- Luxury starts with a loose thread, not marble.
- Health starts with dignity and annoyance, not symptom suffering.

## Topic-To-Route Imagination Pass

After the user chooses a topic, do not immediately script. Generate routes by running at least four different engines:

```text
Chosen topic:
Route A - Genre transplant:
Route B - POV displacement:
Route C - Proof method swap:
Route D - Ritual or standard:
Route E - Counter-category opening:
Recommended route:
Why:
```

Each route must change:

- Who or what sees the story.
- How product truth is proven.
- What image appears in the first 5 seconds.
- What repeated asset the audience remembers.

Reject routes that differ only in scenery, props, or adjectives.

## Route Evaluation

Use this only as a quick route screen. Use `quality-rubric.md` before finalizing a script.

Score each route 1-5:

| Criterion | What to test |
| --- | --- |
| Insight | Does it name a real pressure, not a generic desire? |
| Originality | Have competitors likely made the same film? |
| Product fit | Is the product essential, not decorative? |
| Memory | Is there a repeatable image, line, ritual, or twist? |
| Clarity | Can the audience understand the benefit after one viewing? |
| Production | Can it be shot or generated within constraints? |
| Spread | Would someone describe the idea to another person? |

Revise routes scoring below 4 on product fit or clarity before scripting.

## Minimum Route Diversity

When presenting multiple routes, do not offer minor variants of the same idea. Each route must differ on:

- Audience tension or emotional engine
- Creative leap / metaphor
- Product role
- Key visual
- Ending

If two routes share more than two of these, merge or replace one.

## Strong Route Anatomy

A strong route should fit this sentence:

```text
Because {audience tension},
we dramatize {product truth}
through {unexpected creative device},
so the audience remembers {specific image/line/ritual}
and understands {benefit}.
```

If the sentence feels generic, the route is not ready.
