# Creative Quality Rubric

Use this to judge advertising ideas harshly. The agent must not treat a coherent script as a good script.

## Non-Negotiable Gates

Reject or revise any idea that fails one of these:

1. **Brand/product indispensability**: the brand, product, or category cannot be swapped with an unrelated category or competitor without damaging the selected communication job.
2. **Communication-job fit**: the idea knows whether it is brand building, product marketing, trust/proof, launch, performance, or hybrid.
3. **Sceneable insight**: the insight can be shown in a specific human moment, not only stated in VO.
4. **Memory device**: the audience can recall at least one image, line, ritual, character, or twist.
5. **Benefit or brand bridge**: the creative leap clearly points back to a product benefit, brand truth, category entry point, or distinctive brand asset.
6. **Category freshness**: the idea avoids or meaningfully subverts obvious category cliches.
7. **Imagination engine**: the idea contains a world rule or conceptual machine, not just mood, poetry, or decoration.
8. **Imagination continuity**: the world rule transforms every major beat, especially the middle.
9. **Visual clarity**: the script can be storyboarded into specific shots without relying on abstract VO.
10. **Mode fit**: high-concept scripts commit to a world rule; grounded cinematic scripts commit to believable pressure, observed behavior, real detail, and restrained film craft.
11. **Character and scene realism**: people and places carry life beyond the frame through social context, behavior, blocking, set dressing, and used details.
12. **Grounded imagination**: realistic cinematic work still has one memorable real-world device; it is not just premium montage, sunlight, texture, and restrained acting.
13. **AI keyframe clarity**: generated-image prompts are decomposed into single decisive moments rather than overloaded multi-action scenes.
14. **AI keyframe timing**: image-generation rows use exact timepoints or intervals no longer than 2 seconds; 6-8 second director shots are never used as image prompts.
15. **Motion and transition grammar**: every keyframe has camera movement intent, motion handoff, transition in, and transition out.
16. **Storyboard split clarity**: every storyboard row intended for AI generation states how many images to generate, what each image is, and how images hand off or transition.
17. **Execution path**: the idea can be filmed, animated, or generated with available constraints.

If an idea fails brand/product indispensability or the benefit/brand bridge, kill it. Do not polish it.

## Scorecard

Score 1-5. A final route should average 4+ and score at least 4 on product fit and clarity.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Insight | Generic desire | Recognizable tension | Specific, uncomfortable, true human contradiction |
| Originality | Familiar category ad | Fresh angle but known shape | Feels surprising yet inevitable |
| Imagination | Warm metaphor or decoration | One strong visual twist | Ownable world rule or campaign mythology |
| Imagination continuity | Strong opening, ordinary middle | Several transformed beats | Every major beat is governed by the world rule |
| Product/brand fit | Product pasted on or brand incidental | Product/brand helps the story | Product/brand is necessary for the selected communication job |
| Clarity | Needs explanation | Understandable with VO | Clear from visuals and behavior |
| Visual planning | Vague scene descriptions | Some shot logic | Clear shot size, angle, movement, composition, product placement, transition |
| AI keyframes | 6-8 second scenes stuffed into one prompt | Some split frames | Exact timepoint or max 2-second setup/action/consequence frames with simple single-moment prompts |
| Image split column | No split count; row hides multiple frames | Some A/B split notes | Every row states generated image count, A/B/C frames, handoff, transition |
| Motion grammar | No camera movement or transition logic | Some movement notes | Clear movement intent, handoff, transition in/out for every important frame |
| Memorability | Nice but forgettable | One decent image | Retellable image/line/ritual/twist |
| Emotional force | Decorative feeling | Mild resonance | Makes the audience feel seen, amused, moved, or provoked |
| Brand fit | Could be anyone | Fits the category | Fits this brand's role and tone |
| Mode fit | Wrong style for the ask | Style mostly fits | Style deeply shapes script, shots, performance, sound, and product role |
| Character/scene realism | Generic people in generic places | Some believable details | Specific life rhythm, blocking, props, wardrobe logic, used space, and unsaid pressure |
| Grounded imagination | Pretty realistic montage | One interesting real detail | One disciplined real-world device creates memory, pressure, and product meaning |
| Cultural spread | No reason to share | Has a hook | People could describe/copy/debate it |
| Production feasibility | Vague or impossible | Possible with effort | Clear execution path |
| AI feasibility | Prompt-hostile | Requires careful control | Can be generated through stable frames/prompts |

## Quality Bands

- **90-100**: pitchable hero idea. Protect it and refine execution.
- **80-89**: promising. Improve one weak dimension before scripting.
- **70-79**: usable but not yet distinctive. Mutate the concept.
- **60-69**: ordinary. Keep only the strategic insight and rebuild.
- **Below 60**: discard.

## Common False Positives

The model may think these are good. Treat them with suspicion:

- **Poetic but unsellable**: beautiful lines, weak product role.
- **Wrong job discipline**: judging a brand film only as a product demo, or letting a product demo drift into vague brand poetry.
- **Surreal but arbitrary**: impossible images with no benefit bridge.
- **Strategic but dull**: correct proposition, no memory device.
- **Emotional but generic**: could be any family/beauty/finance ad.
- **Premium-looking but empty**: macro shots and music hide lack of idea.
- **Warm but small**: tenderness, future, care, and growth are present, but no strange conceptual engine exists.
- **Creative hat only**: the opening has a big imaginative frame, but the middle is ordinary daily life with clever captions.
- **Example list**: the script lists many use moments instead of escalating one premise.
- **Cause-washing**: borrows social meaning without a credible brand role.
- **VO-dependent**: if muting the film kills the idea, the visual concept is weak.
- **Storyboard fog**: the description sounds evocative but cannot be sketched as a frame.
- **Shot monotony**: every beat uses the same visual scale or generic cinematic language.
- **Product teleportation**: product appears only when the script needs branding, not where the world rule needs cause.
- **Fake realism**: real location and natural light are present, but there is no dramatic pressure or behavioral truth.
- **Lifestyle wallpaper**: pretty real-life footage with no specific conflict, action, or memory asset.
- **Cinematic adjective soup**: "film texture", "natural light", "documentary feel" are named but not translated into shot choices.
- **Natural montage trap**: organic, health, beauty, or premium goods are shown through sunlight, water, wood, moss, slow hands, and soft VO without a specific device or proof logic.
- **Realism without imagination**: everything is believable but no image, ritual, sound, or reveal is worth retelling.
- **Model in a room**: character is described by beauty/style labels, while body habits, hand behavior, wardrobe logic, social context, and unsaid pressure are missing.
- **Background showroom**: location looks premium but has no used details, spatial obstacle, offscreen world, or product's natural place.
- **Prompt overload**: one image prompt asks for multiple actions, time states, emotions, or product moments.
- **Storyboard-to-image mismatch**: director shots are treated as image prompts without decomposition.
- **Long-frame prompt**: an AI image row covers more than 2 seconds or uses a 6-8 second director-shot range.
- **Missing image split column**: storyboard rows do not state how many stills to generate and what each still should contain.
- **Missing motion grammar**: keyframes lack camera movement intent, motion handoff, transition in, or transition out, so generated images cannot connect into video.
- **Trend-chasing**: references a meme/platform behavior without product necessity.

## Red-Team Questions

Ask before finalizing:

1. What is the one sentence someone would use to describe this ad to a friend?
2. What is the one frame they would remember tomorrow?
3. What is the communication job: brand building, product marketing, trust/proof, launch, performance, or hybrid?
4. What is the one product truth, brand belief, or category entry point that makes the idea belong to this brand?
5. What would a skeptical creative director call lazy here?
6. What would the client worry about?
7. What would the audience misunderstand?
8. What part feels like an AI wrote it?
9. What would make it 30% stranger while still clear?
10. What would make it 30% simpler while keeping the magic?
11. If the budget were cut in half, does the idea still survive?
12. What is the impossible world rule? If there is none, why is this not just a mood film?
13. Does the middle still work as plain daily life if the labels are removed? If yes, rebuild it.
14. What changes from beat to beat inside the fantasy system?
15. Can a storyboard artist draw each frame from the description?
16. Does each important shot have planned shot size, camera angle, movement, product placement, and transition?
17. If this is grounded realism, what is the exact human pressure, observed behavior, motivated light source, ambient sound, and unsaid emotion?
18. If this will be used for image generation, which shots must be split into setup/action/consequence keyframes?
19. What proves this person and place existed before the camera arrived?
20. Is this row a director shot or an AI image keyframe?
21. Would this keyframe still work as a single independent image if generated alone?
22. Does any AI image keyframe exceed 2 seconds? If yes, split it.
23. For each storyboard row, how many images should be generated, and what are A/B/C?
24. What is the camera movement intent, motion handoff, transition in, and transition out for this keyframe?
25. If this is realistic cinema, what is the real-world device that makes it more than a mood film?
26. Is sunlight/nature/texture doing a narrative job, or is it only making the frame pretty?
27. What is the counter-image that breaks the category cliche in the first 5 seconds?

## User Disagreement Protocol

When the user says "not creative", "too ordinary", "not good", or similar:

1. Do not defend the prior idea.
2. Identify the likely failure mode: weak insight, familiar shape, low stakes, decorative surrealism, product pasted on, wrong tone, no talk value.
3. Keep at most one useful component from the prior draft.
4. Change the concept engine, not just the setting or wording.
5. Generate 3 new routes using different axes from `anti-sameness.md`.
6. Explain briefly why the new routes are less likely to repeat the same failure.

## Self-Revision Moves

If the idea is clear but dull:
- Add a contradiction, ritual, or reveal.
- Move the scene to a less expected social context.
- Change the protagonist.
- Invent a world law that the product makes possible, breaks, repairs, or proves.

If the idea is original but unclear:
- Add a stronger product demonstration.
- Make the end frame explain the metaphor.
- Reduce the number of surreal rules.

If the opening is imaginative but the middle is ordinary:
- Define one world law that must act on every beat.
- Replace daily examples with consequences of that law.
- Make the product cause, repair, measure, unlock, or complete the law in the middle.
- Cut any beat that only relabels normal life.

If the idea is emotional but generic:
- Add category-specific detail.
- Replace broad family/work/life imagery with a precise behavior.
- Make the brand role more active.

If the idea is strategic but not memorable:
- Invent a phrase, gesture, repeated prop, sonic cue, or impossible image.
- Make the opening and ending rhyme.

If the idea is too hard to produce:
- Convert spectacle into implication.
- Use a single impossible object instead of a full impossible world.
- Make the AI prompt package frame-based rather than continuous-motion dependent.

If the script idea is strong but the visuals are weak:
- Write a visual progression first: disruption, discovery, mechanism, escalation, resolution.
- Assign a narrative job to every shot.
- Change at least one visual variable per beat: scale, angle, motion, focus, color, product position, or transition.
- Use close-ups for causal product actions and wide shots for world-rule consequences.
- Replace abstract copy with a visible transformation.

If the user wants grounded cinematic realism:
- Remove impossible physics, fantasy institutions, and magical world rules unless explicitly requested.
- Replace spectacle with specific behavior under pressure.
- Add real location texture, motivated light, ambient sound, and performance notes.
- Add character/scene bible: social context, body habit, hand behavior, wardrobe logic, used details, spatial blocking, and product's natural place.
- Make the product enter through a believable routine or decision moment.
- Use one understated image or sound as the memory asset.
- If it still feels ordinary, switch to grounded cinematic imagination: choose evidence trail, object witness, single constraint, misread-then-reveal, natural system as drama, private ritual under pressure, or counter-image.

If image prompts are overloaded:
- Split long director shots into exact timepoint or max 2-second keyframes.
- Remove sequential actions from each prompt.
- Keep one subject, one action, one emotional state, one product role per prompt.
- Mark hero, continuity, transition, and end-frame keyframes separately.
- Add camera movement intent, motion handoff, transition in, and transition out to every keyframe before final output.
- Add a `生成画面拆分` column to the storyboard table with generated image count and A/B/C single-image descriptions.
