# Character And Scene Realism

Use this with `cinematic-realism.md` when the user wants stronger realism, better people, richer scenes, or more believable cinematic texture. The goal is to prevent characters from becoming generic models and locations from becoming pretty backgrounds.

## Realism Principle

People feel real when they carry a life beyond the frame. Places feel real when they show use, pressure, history, and imperfect human arrangement.

Do not describe "a stylish woman in a beautiful apartment." Describe a person with a specific day, body habit, wardrobe logic, social pressure, and room that has been lived in.

## Character Bible

Create this before scripting or storyboarding:

```text
Name / role:
Age range:
Occupation / life rhythm:
Social context:
Today’s pressure:
What they want:
What they will not say:
Body habit:
Hand behavior:
Wardrobe logic:
Grooming detail:
Phone / bag / pocket contents:
Relationship to product:
Performance restraint:
What changes by the end:
```

Do not use abstract traits like "confident", "elegant", "tired", or "modern" alone. Translate them into behavior:

- Confident: does not check the mirror twice; leaves one button undone intentionally.
- Tired: rinses a cup and forgets to turn off the tap for one second.
- Anxious: deletes a typed message; measures twice; checks the door latch again.
- Elegant: repairs a loose cuff instead of fussing with a full outfit.

## Scene Bible

Create this before storyboard frames:

```text
Location:
Why this location, not a generic room:
Time of day:
Weather / season:
Primary light source:
Secondary practical light:
Dominant surfaces:
Used details:
Objects that reveal character:
Spatial obstacle:
Foreground / midground / background logic:
Offscreen world:
Ambient sound:
Smell / texture / temperature:
Product's natural place in the scene:
What should not be too perfect:
```

## Set Dressing Rules

Use details that reveal behavior, not decorative clutter:

- Receipt folded into a book.
- Two mugs but only one washed.
- Jacket on chair back because the person came home late.
- Phone face down during an avoided conversation.
- Supermarket basket indentation on the wrist.
- Cosmetic cap not fully closed.
- Child sticker on an otherwise worklike laptop.
- Train ticket, appointment card, crumpled tissue, product seal, spare hair tie.

Avoid:

- Random luxury props.
- Perfectly aligned objects.
- Empty designer rooms.
- Obvious "ad family" furniture.
- Props that do not affect character, action, or brand memory.

## Blocking And Spatial Relationships

People reveal truth by where they stand and what they avoid.

Plan:

- Distance between characters.
- Who is closer to the door, window, sink, product, or phone.
- Whether someone turns fully, half-turns, or keeps their back partly away.
- Whether the product is reached for confidently, hesitantly, routinely, or accidentally.
- Whether a character crosses a threshold, stays outside, or pauses in a doorway.

Use blocking to show unsaid pressure:

- A parent stands between phone advice and the child.
- A woman stops before entering the city street, then sprays perfume.
- A sick person keeps one hand on a cup and one on a work badge.
- A buyer pauses between two shelves, but touches only one pack.

## Performance Notes

Write actable notes, not emotional labels:

Weak:
```text
She looks confident and elegant.
```

Better:
```text
She notices a loose thread on her sleeve, pulls it once, decides not to fix it, and leaves.
```

Weak:
```text
The father is worried.
```

Better:
```text
He reads a message from the family group, locks the phone, unlocks it again, then puts it screen-down before measuring the spoon.
```

## Real Location Texture

Each key scene should include at least three layers:

1. **Human trace**: something recently touched, moved, used, forgotten, or repaired.
2. **Social trace**: work badge, delivery bag, school notice, clinic form, supermarket receipt, family chat, commuter card.
3. **Material trace**: condensation, dust, scratches, steam, fabric crease, wet pavement, worn leather, chipped mug edge.

## Character Image Prompt Additions

For AI keyframes involving people, specify:

- Age range and ethnicity only when relevant.
- Face continuity: face shape, hair, skin texture, expression restraint.
- Wardrobe logic: why they wear this today.
- Body posture and hand behavior.
- One imperfection or lived-in detail.
- What they are not saying.

Avoid:

- "beautiful woman", "handsome man", "perfect family", "model-like".
- Overly polished skin, fashion-catalog poses, symmetrical fake smiles.
- Vague "cinematic emotion".

## Scene Image Prompt Additions

For AI keyframes involving places, specify:

- Real location type.
- Exact time and motivated light source.
- Foreground, midground, background objects.
- Used details and imperfect textures.
- Product's natural placement.
- Offscreen sound or implied world.

Avoid:

- Generic luxury apartment.
- Empty showroom.
- "cozy home" without concrete details.
- Backgrounds that do not affect story.

## Quality Test

Ask:

1. Could this person exist outside the ad?
2. What did they do 10 minutes before this shot?
3. What object in the scene reveals their life?
4. What detail would a real location scout keep?
5. What detail would an art director remove because it is too fake?
6. What is the character hiding or avoiding?
7. Where is the product before and after the shot?
8. Can the actor perform this without saying the feeling?
