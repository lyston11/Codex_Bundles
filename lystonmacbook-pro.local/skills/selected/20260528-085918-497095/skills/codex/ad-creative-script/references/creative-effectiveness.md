# Creative Effectiveness And Brand Memory

Use this to prevent "interesting but useless" ideas. The work must be imaginative, but the imagination must build brand memory and commercial usefulness.

## Source-Based Principles

Condensed from creative effectiveness and marketing science sources:

- **Creative effectiveness**: the idea should be rooted in strategy and create measurable customer or business impact over time, not just win a smile.
- **Creative Effectiveness Ladder**: aim beyond "nice idea"; look for behavior change, sales impact, brand growth, and ideally an enduring creative platform.
- **Fluent device**: build a repeatable character, situation, phrase, sound, world, or ritual that can be recognized quickly across many executions.
- **Distinctive brand asset**: create or use non-name cues such as color, shape, character, sound, packaging, repeated line, or visual world so the brand comes to mind without needing the logo every second.
- **Future buyer memory**: many people seeing the ad are not buying today; the ad must create a memory link to a future buying situation.
- **Craft**: execution matters. Direction, casting, rhythm, sound, visual design, and copy should elevate the idea, not merely decorate it.

## Five-Gate Creative Test

Before finalizing, answer all five:

1. **Retell gate**: Can a normal person describe this ad in one strange sentence?
2. **Asset gate**: What repeatable brand asset does this create or strengthen?
3. **Entry-point gate**: What buying/usage situation will trigger memory of this brand later?
4. **Platform gate**: Can this idea generate 5-10 future executions without becoming repetitive?
5. **Business gate**: What behavior, perception, or choice is this creative meant to shift?

If the answer to asset, entry-point, or platform is weak, the idea is not strong enough no matter how imaginative it sounds.

## Creative Platform Anatomy

A strong campaign platform includes:

```text
Brand role:
World rule:
Repeatable device:
Distinctive assets:
Category entry points:
Episode engine:
Proof bridge:
Tone:
Forbidden repeats:
```

Example structure:

```text
When {audience situation} happens,
the brand's world always responds by {repeatable impossible rule},
revealing {product/brand truth}.
The recurring asset is {character/object/sound/line/visual law}.
```

## Distinctive Asset Menu

Choose at least two assets to build into the script:

- **Character**: recurring role, creature, institution, inspector, host, object narrator.
- **World**: recurring place or system with clear rules.
- **Line**: repeatable phrase people can quote.
- **Sound**: sonic cue, announcement, ritual sound, jingle fragment, mechanical signature.
- **Gesture**: physical behavior viewers can imitate.
- **Object**: spoon, pack, seal, card, tool, doorway, badge, meter, map.
- **Visual law**: gravity bends, rooms resize, doubt materializes, time folds, labels become maps.
- **Color/material**: ownable palette, texture, light behavior, packaging shape.
- **Format**: mockumentary, trial, inspection report, broadcast, field guide, mission log.

Do not create assets that could belong to any brand in the category. Tie them to the product, pack, brand role, or proof.

## Category Entry Points

For each script, define memory triggers:

```text
When the audience is...
- comparing products on shelf
- receiving advice from family
- worrying whether they chose right
- building a daily routine
- facing a specific use moment

They should remember...
- the brand's world/device
- the product role
- the line or image
```

## Imagination With Commercial Discipline

Good weird:
- The impossible rule dramatizes the audience problem.
- The product has a causal role in the world.
- The same rule can repeat in future ads.
- The brand is encoded through assets.

Bad weird:
- Random fantasy unrelated to the purchase situation.
- Strange images that require long explanation.
- A clever film where the brand appears only at the end.
- A surreal world that can fit any brand.
- A one-off stunt with no repeatable memory device.

## Kill Criteria

Reject a route if:

- It has no repeatable asset.
- It cannot be explained in one strange sentence.
- The product is only a prop or sponsor.
- It creates a world but no buying-situation memory.
- It would be remembered as "that cute baby ad" rather than the brand.
- It depends on a generic line such as "for every tomorrow", "grow with love", "protect every step", or "because every choice matters".
