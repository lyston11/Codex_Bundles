# Grounded Cinematic Realism

Use this when the user wants a super-realistic, filmic, premium, documentary-like, grounded, naturalistic, or non-fantasy advertising script and storyboard. This mode is an alternative to high-concept magical world rules.

## Core Idea

Grounded cinematic realism does not mean "plain." It means the drama is carried by believable human behavior, observed detail, natural locations, motivated light, restrained camera language, performance nuance, sound design, and editing rhythm.

The creative leap is not an impossible world rule. It is a precise emotional situation rendered so truthfully that it feels cinematic and memorable.

When the user says a realistic film still lacks imagination, switch to `grounded-cinematic-imagination.md`. Do not solve the problem by adding prettier locations, more golden light, or more poetic VO; solve it with a sharper real-world device.

## When To Use

Use this mode when the user asks for:

- 超真实电影质感
- 真实电影感
- 写实高级感
- 纪录片质感
- 克制、不魔幻、不奇幻
- 像真实品牌片 / 电影短片 / 高级 TVC
- naturalistic / grounded / cinematic realism / documentary realism

Do not force a fantasy premise in this mode. Still keep the idea distinctive through situation, casting, behavior, rhythm, and brand memory.

Reject the idea if it is only:

- Beautiful people in beautiful light.
- A sentimental montage.
- A generic family/lifestyle scene.
- A manifesto VO over stock-like footage.
- A product demo with cinematic adjectives.
- A realistic setting but no dramatic pressure.
- A realistic-looking character with no life beyond the frame.
- A real location used as a pretty background rather than a pressure-bearing space.
- Nature, city, home, or luxury footage that could be swapped into any premium ad without changing the story.

For character and scene specificity, use `character-scene-realism.md`.

## Realistic Script Engine

Build from small pressures, not plot twists:

1. **Pressure**: What tiny real-life pressure is happening?
2. **Avoidance**: What is the character not saying?
3. **Behavior**: What do they do instead of explaining?
4. **Product role**: How does the product naturally enter the routine?
5. **Shift**: What small visible change happens?
6. **Aftertaste**: What image lingers after the scene ends?

Example:

```text
Not: "A mother loves her child."
Better: A mother deletes a message asking for advice, then measures the spoon twice because she wants to trust her own routine.
```

## Cinematic Realism Visual Rules

- Use real locations with texture: kitchen counter scratches, humid bathroom mirror, supermarket shelf noise, elevator light, morning window glare, child's sock on chair.
- Use motivated lighting: window light, practical lamps, refrigerator light, phone screen, streetlight, kitchen range hood, bathroom mirror light.
- Use restrained camera movement: static frames, slow push-ins, observational handheld, gentle tracking, match cuts based on behavior.
- Use shallow depth of field only when it supports attention, not as automatic "cinematic" decoration.
- Use natural sound: kettle click, spoon tapping ceramic, pack seal opening, child breathing, traffic outside, phone vibration, distant neighbor noise.
- Let actors underplay. Avoid ad-smiles, perfect reactions, and obvious tearful catharsis.
- Let the product sit in the real world: on a counter, in a bag, beside receipts, next to a half-finished note, under imperfect light.

## Cinematic Realism Shot Progression

Use this structure for a 45-90s brand film:

1. **Observation**: quiet, specific real-life moment.
2. **Pressure accumulates**: small frictions appear through behavior, not explanation.
3. **Routine begins**: product enters naturally.
4. **Micro-shift**: a subtle emotional or practical change becomes visible.
5. **Human landing**: no big speech; one gesture reveals the point.
6. **Brand frame**: product and brand line emerge from the same real environment.

## Brand Memory Without Fantasy

Create memory through:

- A repeated gesture: measuring twice, folding a receipt, muting advice, touching a pack seal, checking a message but not sending it.
- A repeated sound: spoon on cup, kettle click, pack seal, elevator ding, phone vibration.
- A repeated frame: product seen in real routine from the same angle.
- A precise line: short, human, not slogan-heavy.
- A category entry point: shelf decision, night routine, family advice, busy morning, first return to work, travel packing, illness onset, etc.

## Quality Test

Ask:

1. Could this happen in a real home, store, clinic, office, or street?
2. Is there a specific pressure, not just "life is busy"?
3. Can the scene be understood with muted VO?
4. Is the product role natural but necessary?
5. Are there at least 5 concrete sensory details?
6. Does the ending avoid forced uplift?
7. Would it still feel premium without slow motion?
8. Is every shot motivated by emotion, action, or information?

## Avoid

- Random lens jargon without narrative purpose.
- Over-polished spotless homes.
- Smiling stock families.
- Everyone speaking in brand slogans.
- Big VO explaining obvious feelings.
- Magic realism, surreal metaphors, fantasy institutions, or impossible physics unless the user asks to blend modes.
- "Film look" clichés: anamorphic flare, rain window, golden dust, shallow focus everywhere.
- Organic/nature cliches: first sunbeam through trees, child touching moss, clear stream purity proof, wooden table packshot, scoop glowing in sunlight, and generic "nature/patience/growth" VO unless a sharper realistic device subverts them.
