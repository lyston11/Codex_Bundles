# Imagination Engine

Use this when an idea feels polished but small. The goal is not "more poetic"; the goal is a stranger, more ownable world rule that still sells.

## Imagination Definition

In advertising, imagination is not decoration. It is a conceptual machine:

```text
Real audience tension + impossible world rule + product-specific bridge = memorable ad idea
```

If the impossible element can be removed and the script still works, it is decoration, not imagination.

If the impossible element cannot become a repeatable brand asset, campaign format, or future buying memory, it is a one-off trick, not a strong advertising idea.

If the impossible rule appears only in the opening and closing while the middle becomes ordinary product usage or daily-life montage, the script fails. The world rule must transform every major beat.

## Required Leap

For high-concept work, generate at least 5 impossible premises before choosing one. Each premise must answer:

- What impossible rule governs the world?
- Why does this impossible rule dramatize the audience tension?
- Why does only this product/category credibly resolve or complete the rule?
- What is the single frame someone would retell?
- What could become a campaign platform, not just one film?
- What distinctive brand asset would repeat across future executions?
- What category entry point would trigger memory of this idea later?

## Imagination Axes

Force the idea through at least two axes:

| Axis | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Scale rupture | What if a tiny daily act controlled a city, planet, courtroom, museum, weather system, or civilization? |
| Agency shift | What if the hero is not the parent/user, but the spoon, formula powder, fridge light, receipt, baby monitor, shadow, future room, or family rumor? |
| World law | What new law exists in this universe? Who enforces it? What breaks if the product is absent? |
| Genre theft | What if the category becomes a heist, trial, space mission, monster film, sports broadcast, auction, nature documentary, or disaster drill? |
| Material metaphor | What physical material can the benefit become: gravity, weather, architecture, sound, time, maps, scaffolding, constellation, armor, oxygen? |
| Social ritual | What behavior could people copy, parody, challenge, or quote? |
| Temporal twist | What if the future audits the present, the past negotiates with today, or one minute expands into a lifetime? |
| Forbidden perspective | What point of view would category ads never use but audiences understand instantly? |

## Imagination Levels

Use this ladder. Do not present Level 1-2 as premium creative.

| Level | Description | Example shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Literal sell | Product benefit explained plainly. | Better nutrition, better care. |
| 2. Warm metaphor | Gentle symbols around growth/future/care. | Letters from tomorrow, glowing diary, soft nest. |
| 3. Visual twist | One memorable impossible image. | A spoon briefly bends gravity in the kitchen. |
| 4. World rule | The film runs on a new logic. | Every parent's doubt is weighed by a tiny inspection bureau. |
| 5. Brand mythology | A repeatable universe or ritual. | The brand operates a secret "Uncertainty Department" that helps families turn doubt into daily care. |

For big creative asks, aim for Level 4-5.

## Weak Imagination Warning Signs

Reject:

- Beautifully written warmth with no strange rule.
- Future letters, glowing memories, dream rooms, soft stars, or diaries unless radically subverted.
- A child growing up montage as the main idea.
- Abstract particles, light ribbons, or floating nutrition icons as the imagination.
- A metaphor that could fit any milk powder, diaper, education, or insurance brand.
- A film where the most interesting thing is the VO.

## Impossible Premise Starters

Use and mutate:

1. **The Bureau**: A hidden institution manages the invisible emotional work around the product.
2. **The Trial**: A daily habit is put on trial by an unexpected judge.
3. **The Weather System**: The product changes not mood but the physical weather of a room, street, or city.
4. **The Heist**: The audience is stealing back time, calm, sleep, confidence, or a small ordinary future.
5. **The Museum**: Ordinary objects become priceless artifacts of care.
6. **The Monster**: A category anxiety becomes a visible monster, but not in a fearmongering way.
7. **The Sport**: A private routine is broadcast like a high-stakes event.
8. **The Embassy**: Two worlds negotiate: parent and child, today and tomorrow, science and instinct.
9. **The Factory**: Invisible trust is manufactured, inspected, rejected, and repaired.
10. **The Map**: The product turns a chaotic invisible journey into navigable terrain.

## Selection Test

Choose the premise that best passes:

- Can I explain it in one weird sentence?
- Can I see the poster?
- Can it generate 5 more films?
- Does it dramatize a true audience tension, not just a clever trick?
- Is the product more than a sponsor of the fantasy?
- Would a normal person retell it without advertising vocabulary?
- Would they remember the brand or only the weird scene?

## Imagination Density Test

For each major beat, fill this before scripting:

```text
Beat:
Ordinary action underneath:
How the world rule transforms it:
What impossible image happens:
How the product participates causally:
What memory asset repeats:
Why this is not just daily life:
```

Reject a beat if "ordinary action underneath" is the only meaningful content.

## 70 Percent Rule

In a high-imagination script, at least 70% of screen time must be governed by the impossible premise. "Governed" means the scene logic, stakes, visuals, sound, and behavior are changed by the premise.

Acceptable:
- Feeding becomes a customs checkpoint, mission refuel, weather calibration, or treaty ritual under a clear world law.
- Bathtime, stairs, dog greeting, or sleep can appear only if transformed into a new rule-driven event with stakes and surprise.

Not acceptable:
- The opening names a secret bureau, then the middle shows normal family milestones with humorous labels.
- Everyday tasks are merely captioned as missions.
- A surreal control room watches ordinary footage without changing it.

## Beat Escalation

Each beat should escalate one of:

- The world rule becomes clearer.
- The stakes become stranger.
- The product's role becomes more necessary.
- The recurring asset mutates in a memorable way.
- The audience discovers a new consequence of the premise.

If consecutive beats merely list new examples, rebuild the middle.

## Platform Stress Test

Before writing a full script, create 3 quick episode titles for the same idea. If this is hard, the idea may be a one-off.

Example format:

```text
Platform:
World rule:
Recurring asset:
Episode 1:
Episode 2:
Episode 3:
Buying situation memory:
```

Reject the premise if the future episodes are just the same joke in different locations.

## Weirdness Discipline

Make it weirder by changing the world law, not by adding more visual noise.

Prefer:
- One impossible rule followed rigorously.
- One recurring institution, character, format, or ritual.
- One clear product bridge.
- One memorable line or sonic cue.

Avoid:
- Many unrelated surreal images.
- A cinematic dream sequence with no rules.
- A clever twist that ends before the brand enters.
- Art direction pretending to be an idea.
